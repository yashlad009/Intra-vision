# 15. Main Idea Questions

One of the most common RC question types is identifying the **Main Idea**.

The main idea is the central message that the author wants to communicate.

It is **not** a single sentence but the overall purpose of the passage.

---

## Example Passage

Artificial Intelligence is transforming industries such as healthcare, education, finance, and transportation. Although AI improves efficiency and productivity, ethical concerns regarding privacy and job displacement continue to grow.

### Question

What is the main idea?

Options

A. AI only benefits healthcare.

B. AI improves industries but creates ethical concerns.

C. AI should replace human workers.

D. Privacy is the only issue with AI.

### Solution

The passage discusses both the advantages and concerns of AI.

Answer

**B**

---

## Tips

✔ Read the first and last paragraphs carefully.

✔ Notice repeated ideas.

✔ Ignore minor details.

---

# 16. Inference Questions

Inference questions require you to identify information that is **implied**, not directly stated.

You must think logically based on the passage.

---

## Example

### Passage

The company introduced flexible working hours, and employee productivity increased significantly over the next six months.

### Question

Which statement can be inferred?

A. Flexible working hours may improve productivity.

B. All companies should remove offices.

C. Employees worked fewer hours.

D. Productivity always depends on salary.

### Solution

The passage suggests a possible relationship between flexibility and productivity.

Answer

**A**

---

## Important Rule

Inference means

**Reading Between the Lines**

Do not add your own assumptions.

---

# 17. Author's Tone

The **tone** describes the author's attitude toward the topic.

Understanding tone helps answer many placement questions.

---

## Common Tone Types

### Positive

The author appreciates the subject.

Example Words

- beneficial
- successful
- valuable
- encouraging

---

### Negative

The author criticizes the subject.

Example Words

- harmful
- disappointing
- dangerous
- ineffective

---

### Neutral

Only facts are presented.

No emotion.

---

### Optimistic

The author expects good outcomes.

---

### Pessimistic

The author expects negative outcomes.

---

### Critical

The author evaluates and points out weaknesses.

---

### Analytical

The author objectively examines facts and evidence.

---

### Persuasive

The author tries to convince the reader.

---

## Example

"The government has introduced several reforms. While these reforms have improved infrastructure, implementation remains inconsistent."

Tone

Balanced and Analytical

---

## Memory Tip

Tone reflects **how** the author writes, not **what** the author writes.

---

# 18. Vocabulary Questions

Many RC questions ask the meaning of a word **based on context**, not dictionary definitions.

---

## Example

"The scientist's discovery was revolutionary."

Question

The word "revolutionary" most nearly means

A. Ordinary

B. Groundbreaking

C. Confusing

D. Temporary

Answer

**B**

---

## Context Clues

Use nearby words to determine meaning.

Look for

- Examples
- Contrasts
- Definitions
- Synonyms

---

## Example

"The journey was arduous, requiring several days of continuous walking."

Meaning of "arduous"

The surrounding words indicate difficulty.

Answer

Difficult

---

# 19. Fact vs Opinion

Many students confuse facts with opinions.

Understanding the difference is essential.

---

## Fact

A statement that can be verified.

Example

"The Earth revolves around the Sun."

---

## Opinion

A personal belief or judgment.

Example

"Summer is the best season."

---

## Example Passage

Electric vehicles reduce pollution compared to traditional fuel vehicles. However, some experts believe their batteries require further improvement.

Fact

Electric vehicles reduce pollution.

Opinion

Battery technology requires further improvement.

---

## Tip

Facts can usually be verified.

Opinions express beliefs or evaluations.

---

# 20. True / False / Cannot Say

These questions test your understanding of the passage.

---

## True

Directly supported by the passage.

---

## False

Contradicts the passage.

---

## Cannot Say

Insufficient information is available.

---

## Example

### Passage

The company hired 500 engineers during 2025.

Question

The company hired 600 engineers.

Answer

False

---

Question

The company will hire more engineers next year.

Answer

Cannot Say

---

## Rule

Never guess future events unless the passage explicitly states them.

---

# 21. Paragraph Mapping

Paragraph Mapping helps understand long passages quickly.

Instead of memorizing everything,

summarize each paragraph in one short sentence.

---

## Example

Paragraph 1

Introduction to climate change.

Paragraph 2

Causes of climate change.

Paragraph 3

Effects on agriculture.

Paragraph 4

Possible solutions.

---

Now, when a question asks about agriculture,

you immediately know where to look.

---

## Benefits

✔ Saves time

✔ Improves accuracy

✔ Prevents rereading the entire passage

---

# 22. Critical Reading

Critical Reading involves evaluating the author's arguments.

Instead of accepting every statement,

you analyze

- Evidence
- Logic
- Assumptions
- Conclusions

---

## Example

### Statement

"Online education is better than classroom education because students save travel time."

Question

Is the argument complete?

Answer

No.

Saving travel time alone does not prove online education is better.

Other factors such as interaction, discipline, and practical learning must also be considered.

---

## Tip

Ask yourself

"Does the evidence fully support the conclusion?"

---

# 23. Elimination Technique

Often,

finding the correct answer is easier by eliminating incorrect options.

---

## Remove Options That

❌ Contradict the passage.

❌ Introduce new information.

❌ Use extreme words like

Always

Never

Everyone

Nobody

unless supported.

❌ Change the author's meaning.

---

## Example

If two options seem correct,

choose the one that is directly supported by the passage.

---

Remote work has become increasingly common due to technological advancements. Many organizations have reported increased employee satisfaction and reduced operational costs. However, some managers believe that prolonged remote work can reduce collaboration and innovation if proper communication systems are not maintained.

Remote work offers several advantages but also presents challenges.

Communication plays an important role in successful remote work.

### Question 3

Answer

Balanced and Analytical

Remote work is effective when organizations maintain proper communication.

# 25. Speed Reading Techniques

Reading quickly does **not** mean reading carelessly.

The goal is to improve both speed and understanding.

---

## Technique 1

Read in phrases,

not word by word.

---

## Technique 2

Avoid moving your lips while reading.

(Subvocalization slows reading.)

---

## Technique 3

Train your eyes to move smoothly across lines.

---

## Technique 4

Focus on keywords

such as

because,

however,

therefore,

although,

in contrast.

---

## Technique 5

Practice reading newspapers, editorials, and articles daily.

---

# 26. Shortcut Techniques

### Shortcut 1

Read the questions first if the passage is very long.

---

### Shortcut 2

Underline names, dates, and keywords.

---

### Shortcut 3

Summarize each paragraph mentally.

---

### Shortcut 4

Do not spend too much time on one difficult question.

---

### Shortcut 5

Eliminate wrong options before selecting the final answer.

---

### Shortcut 6

For vocabulary questions,

use surrounding words as clues.

## Passage

Artificial Intelligence is increasingly being used in healthcare to assist doctors in diagnosing diseases, analyzing medical images, and predicting patient outcomes. While AI improves efficiency and accuracy, experts emphasize that it should support rather than replace medical professionals. Human judgment, ethical decision-making, and empathy remain essential components of quality healthcare.

---

### Question 1

What is the central idea?

A. AI should replace doctors.

B. AI improves healthcare but cannot completely replace human doctors.

C. Healthcare no longer requires professionals.

D. AI is harmful.

Answer

**B**

---

### Question 2

Which statement is supported?

A. AI eliminates the need for hospitals.

B. Human judgment remains important.

C. AI never makes mistakes.

D. Patients dislike AI.

Answer

**B**

---

### Question 3

Author's Tone

Answer

Balanced and Analytical

---

### Question 4

Which inference follows?

Answer

AI should be used as a supporting tool in healthcare.

# 37. Interview Questions

### Q1

How can Reading Comprehension be improved?

Answer

By reading daily, expanding vocabulary, and practicing inference-based questions.

---

### Q2

What is the difference between a fact and an inference?

Answer

A fact is directly stated.

An inference is logically derived from the given information.

---

### Q3

What is the author's tone?

Answer

The author's attitude toward the subject.

---

### Q4

Why should outside knowledge be avoided?

Answer

Because RC answers must be based only on the passage.

---

### Q5

What is the best strategy for long passages?

Answer

Identify the main idea of each paragraph, underline keywords, and eliminate unsupported options.
