# 5. Basic Terminology

Before solving questions, you must understand four important terms.

---

## 5.1 Cost Price (CP)

Cost Price is the price at which a shopkeeper purchases an item.

It is usually represented by

CP

Example

A shopkeeper buys a bag for ₹800.

Cost Price = ₹800

Think of Cost Price as:

**"Buying Price"**

---

## 5.2 Selling Price (SP)

Selling Price is the price at which the item is sold to the customer.

It is represented by

SP

Example

The same bag is sold for ₹1000.

Selling Price = ₹1000

Think of Selling Price as:

**"Selling Price"**

---

## 5.3 Profit

If the Selling Price is greater than the Cost Price,

then the shopkeeper earns Profit.

Condition

SP > CP

Example

CP = ₹500

SP = ₹650

Profit

=650−500

=₹150

The shopkeeper earned ₹150.

---

## 5.4 Loss

If the Selling Price is less than the Cost Price,

then the shopkeeper suffers Loss.

Condition

SP < CP

Example

CP = ₹900

SP = ₹750

Loss

=900−750

=₹150

The shopkeeper lost ₹150.

---

## 5.5 No Profit No Loss

If

SP = CP

there is neither profit nor loss.

Example

CP = ₹1000

SP = ₹1000

Profit = ₹0

Loss = ₹0

# 7. Core Formulas

These formulas should be memorized.

## Formula 1

Profit

= Selling Price − Cost Price

Profit = SP − CP

---

## Formula 2

Loss

= Cost Price − Selling Price

Loss = CP − SP

---

## Formula 3

Profit Percentage

Profit%

=(Profit÷Cost Price)×100

Notice carefully:

The denominator is **Cost Price**, not Selling Price.

---

## Formula 4

Loss Percentage

Loss%

=(Loss÷Cost Price)×100

Again,

always divide by Cost Price.

---

## Formula 5

Selling Price

SP

=CP+Profit

---

## Formula 6

Selling Price

SP

=CP−Loss

# 8. Remember This Rule Forever

Almost every beginner makes this mistake.

Whenever Profit% or Loss% is asked,

ALWAYS divide by

Cost Price.

Never divide by Selling Price.

Easy way to remember:

> Profit and Loss always belong to the person who purchased the item.

Therefore,

everything is compared with the buying price (Cost Price).

# 13. Finding Selling Price (SP)

Sometimes the Cost Price and Profit Percentage are given.

You need to find the Selling Price.

Instead of finding Profit first and then adding it, use the direct formula.

## Formula

Selling Price

= Cost Price × (100 + Profit%) / 100

---

₹2,300

# 45. Frequently Asked Interview Questions

### Why is Cost Price used while calculating Profit Percentage?

Because Profit or Loss is measured relative to the amount invested to purchase the item.

---

### Can Profit and Discount occur together?

Yes.

A shopkeeper may first increase the Marked Price,

then offer a Discount,

and still earn a Profit.

---

### Can Selling Price be greater than Marked Price?

Normally no.

However,

it is possible in exceptional situations like additional service charges or taxes applied separately.
