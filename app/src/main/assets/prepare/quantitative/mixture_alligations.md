# 6. What is Allegation?

Allegation is a shortcut technique used to determine

the ratio in which two different quantities should be mixed

to obtain a desired average.

Instead of solving lengthy equations,

we use a simple cross method.

This method is known as

> Rule of Allegation

It is one of the fastest methods in placement aptitude.

---

₹40/kg

Tea B

₹60/kg

₹50/kg

| Physical process | Mathematical technique |

 # 14. Rule of Allegation (Complete Explanation)

The **Rule of Allegation** is one of the fastest methods used to determine the ratio in which two substances should be mixed to obtain a desired average.

Instead of forming lengthy equations,

we simply compare the difference between the values.

This method is widely used in placement exams because it saves a lot of time.

---

## The Basic Idea

Suppose we have two types of rice.

Rice A

₹40/kg

Rice B

₹60/kg

Required Mixture

₹50/kg

Question

How much of each rice should be mixed?

Instead of using equations,

we apply the Rule of Allegation.

---

# 15. Cross (Diagonal) Method

This is the heart of the Allegation chapter.

Draw the values diagonally.

```
       Higher Value
          ₹60
            \
             \
      ₹50 (Mean)
             /
            /
       ₹40
     Lower Value
```

Now subtract diagonally.

Higher − Mean

60 − 50 = 10

Mean − Lower

50 − 40 = 10

Therefore,

Required Ratio

=

10 : 10

=

1 : 1

Answer

Mix equal quantities.

---

## Memory Trick

Never subtract vertically.

Always subtract diagonally.

Higher

↓

Mean

↓

Lower

Cross subtraction

↓

Required Ratio

---

# 16. Why Cross Subtraction Works

Many students memorize the diagram but never understand why it works.

Imagine,

Rice costing ₹60 is expensive.

Rice costing ₹40 is cheap.

If the required average is close to ₹60,

then more cheap rice must be mixed to reduce the average.

Similarly,

if the average is closer to ₹40,

more expensive rice is required to increase the average.

Hence,

the quantity is always inversely proportional to the difference.

This is why

Cross Subtraction works.

 # 29. Repeated Replacement Problems

Repeated Replacement is one of the most interesting applications of Mixture and Allegation.

In these questions,

a certain quantity of liquid is removed from a container,

and an equal quantity of another liquid is added.

This process may be repeated several times.

These questions are commonly asked in:

- Banking Exams
- SSC
- Campus Placements
- MBA Entrance Tests

---

## Standard Formula

If

A container initially contains

V litres.

Every time,

x litres are removed

and replaced.

The process is repeated

n times.

Then,

Quantity Remaining

=

Original Quantity ×

(1 − x/V)^n

---

## Memory Trick

Think

Remove

↓

Replace

↓

Repeat

Every replacement reduces the original liquid.

---

## Example 1

A container has

40 litres

of Milk.

10 litres

are removed

Find the quantity of Milk left.

### Solution

Milk Remaining

40

×

(1−10/40)^2

=

40

×

(3/4)^2

=

40×

9/16

=

22.5 litres

Answer

22.5 litres

# 40. Key Takeaways

• A **Mixture** is formed by combining two or more substances.

• **Allegation** is a mathematical shortcut used to determine the mixing ratio.

• The average value of a mixture always lies between the highest and lowest values.

• If equal quantities are mixed, the average is simply the arithmetic mean.

• If quantities are unequal, use the weighted average formula.

• The Rule of Allegation is one of the fastest methods for solving placement-level mixture questions.

• In Milk and Water problems, water is generally assumed to have zero cost unless specified otherwise.

• Repeated Replacement questions follow a standard formula and appear regularly in competitive exams.
