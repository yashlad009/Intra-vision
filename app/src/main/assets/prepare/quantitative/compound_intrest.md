# 1. Introduction

Compound Interest (CI) is one of the most important topics in Quantitative Aptitude, Banking Exams, Campus Placements and Financial Mathematics.

Unlike Simple Interest, where interest is calculated only on the original Principal, Compound Interest is calculated on the **Principal plus the previously earned Interest**.

Because of this,

the amount grows faster every year.

This concept is often called

> **Interest on Interest**

or

> **Compounding**

Understanding Compound Interest is essential because it appears in almost every financial activity today.

Examples include:

- Fixed Deposits (FD)
- Recurring Deposits (RD)
- Home Loans
- Personal Loans
- Education Loans
- Credit Cards
- Mutual Funds
- Investments
- Retirement Planning
- Banking Products

Most placement companies ask at least one Compound Interest question because it tests:

- Percentage
- Exponents
- Logical Thinking
- Financial Understanding

# 4. What is Compound Interest?

Compound Interest means

the interest of one year becomes part of the Principal for the next year.

This is the biggest difference between Simple Interest and Compound Interest.

Think of it like this.

Year 1

Principal

↓

Interest Added

↓

New Amount

↓

Year 2 starts with this New Amount

↓

Again Interest Added

↓

Even Bigger Amount

This process continues every year.

Therefore,

interest itself starts earning interest.

This is why Compound Interest grows faster.

# 12. Why the Formula Works

Many students memorize the formula without understanding it.

Let's derive it.

Suppose

Principal

= ₹100

Rate

=10%

---

### After First Year

Interest

=₹10

Amount

=₹110

Which is

100×1.10

---

### After Second Year

New Principal

=₹110

Interest

=₹11

Amount

=₹121

Which is

100×1.10×1.10

=100×1.10²

---

### After Third Year

Amount

=121×1.10

=133.10

Which is

100×1.10³

Hence,

Amount

=P(1+R/100)^T

The power appears because multiplication happens every year.

This is why Compound Interest is called **exponential growth**.

# 17. Difference Between Simple Interest and Compound Interest

This is one of the most important interview as well as placement concepts.

| Simple Interest | Compound Interest |
|-----------------|------------------|
| Interest only on Principal | Interest on Principal + Previous Interest |
| Equal interest every year | Increasing interest every year |
| Linear growth | Exponential growth |
| Easy calculations | Slightly more calculations |
| Mostly short-term | Long-term investments |

# 21. Half-Yearly Compounding

Until now, we assumed that interest was compounded **once every year**.

However, banks and financial institutions may compound interest:

- Half-Yearly (Every 6 Months)
- Quarterly (Every 3 Months)
- Monthly
- Daily

The more frequently the interest is compounded,

the greater the final Amount becomes.

---

## What is Half-Yearly Compounding?

In Half-Yearly Compounding,

interest is calculated **twice every year**.

Instead of waiting one year,

the bank adds interest after every six months.

This means:

- Number of Compounding Periods doubles.
- Rate of Interest becomes half.

# 45. Key Takeaways

• Compound Interest is calculated on both the original Principal and the accumulated Interest.

• The master formula **A = P(1 + R/100)^T** is the foundation of almost every Compound Interest problem.

• Always calculate the **Amount first**, then subtract the Principal to obtain Compound Interest.

• More frequent compounding (Half-Yearly, Quarterly, Monthly) always results in a larger Amount than Annual Compounding.

• Compound Interest represents **exponential growth**, while Simple Interest represents **linear growth**.

• For one year, **Simple Interest and Compound Interest are always equal**.

• The shortcut **Difference = PR²/10000** is valid **only for 2 years**.

• Understanding Compound Interest helps in solving banking, investment, loan, inflation, and business-growth problems.
