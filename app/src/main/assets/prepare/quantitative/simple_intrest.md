# 1. Introduction

Simple Interest (SI) is one of the most important financial topics in Quantitative Aptitude. It is frequently asked in placement examinations because it combines Percentage, Multiplication, Division and Logical Thinking.

Whenever money is borrowed or invested for a fixed period, interest is charged. If the interest is calculated only on the original amount throughout the entire duration, it is called **Simple Interest**.

Unlike Compound Interest, the interest does not earn additional interest.

This makes Simple Interest one of the easiest yet highest-scoring topics in aptitude.

---

## What is Interest?

Suppose you borrow ₹10,000 from a bank.

The bank is providing you money today.

In return, after one year, the bank expects:

- Original Amount
- Extra Amount

That extra amount is called **Interest**.

Example

Borrowed Amount = ₹10,000

Interest = ₹800

Amount Returned

= ₹10,800

Here,

₹800 is the reward paid for using someone else's money.

---

## Real-Life Example

Imagine your friend lends you ₹5,000 for one year.

He says,

> "Return ₹5,500 after one year."

Extra Money

₹500

is the Interest.

Original Money

₹5,000

is called the Principal.

Total Returned

₹5,500

is called the Amount.

---

## Why Banks Charge Interest?

Banks lend money to customers.

If banks never charged interest,

they would never make profit.

Interest helps banks:

- Earn income
- Cover inflation
- Cover lending risk
- Grow deposits
- Provide more loans

Similarly,

when you keep money in a Fixed Deposit,

the bank pays YOU interest.

# 9. Finding Principal (P)

Sometimes the examiner gives you:

- Simple Interest
- Rate of Interest
- Time

and asks you to find the **Principal**.

Instead of guessing, use the direct formula.

---

## Formula

Principal (P)

=

(SI × 100)

÷

(R × T)

Where,

- SI = Simple Interest
- R = Rate (% per annum)
- T = Time (Years)

---

## How to Remember?

From the SI formula,

SI = (P × R × T) / 100

Move P to one side,

P = (SI × 100) / (R × T)

Think:

> **Interest ×100 ÷ (Rate × Time)**

# 19. Comparison Between Simple Interest and Compound Interest

| Simple Interest | Compound Interest |
|-----------------|------------------|
| Calculated only on Principal | Calculated on Principal + Previous Interest |
| Same Interest every year | Interest changes every year |
| Easy calculations | Comparatively complex |
| Linear Growth | Exponential Growth |
| Mostly short-term loans | Investments and long-term loans |

---

## Example

Principal = ₹10,000

Rate = 10%

Time = 3 Years

Simple Interest

Year 1 = ₹1,000

Year 2 = ₹1,000

Year 3 = ₹1,000

Total SI

₹3,000

Compound Interest

Year 1 = ₹1,000

Year 2 = ₹1,100

Year 3 = ₹1,210

Total CI

₹3,310

Notice that Compound Interest is always greater than or equal to Simple Interest (when Rate > 0).

# 22. Interview Questions

### Q1. What is Simple Interest?

Simple Interest is the interest calculated only on the original Principal throughout the investment or loan period.

---

### Q2. Why is it called Simple Interest?

Because interest is always calculated on the initial Principal and does not earn interest on previous interest.

---

### Q3. Can Simple Interest change every year?

No.

If Principal, Rate and Time remain unchanged,

the interest earned every year remains the same.

---

### Q4. Which is greater?

Simple Interest or Compound Interest?

For the same Principal, Rate and Time,

Compound Interest is always greater than or equal to Simple Interest.

---

### Q5. Where is Simple Interest used?
