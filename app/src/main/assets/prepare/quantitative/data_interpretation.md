# Part 1 — Fundamentals of Data Interpretation

# 1. Introduction

Data is everywhere.

Every day, companies collect millions of records about

- Customers
- Sales
- Revenue
- Expenses
- Population
- Weather
- Cricket Scores
- Website Visitors
- Stock Markets

However,

raw numbers alone do not help in making decisions.

The ability to read,

understand,

analyze,

and interpret numerical information

is called

**Data Interpretation (DI).**

Data Interpretation is one of the most important topics in Quantitative Aptitude because it tests not only mathematical ability but also analytical thinking and decision-making skills.

Unlike traditional mathematics,

DI focuses on extracting meaningful conclusions from graphs, charts, tables, and reports.

# 5. What is Data Interpretation?

Data Interpretation means

collecting,

organizing,

understanding,

and analyzing numerical information

to answer questions and make decisions.

Instead of solving direct equations,

you first understand the information

and then perform calculations.

---

## Simple Example

Suppose a company sells

| Month | Sales |
|--------|-------:|
| January | 120 |
| February | 150 |
| March | 180 |

Questions may ask

- Which month had the highest sales?

Answer

March

---

- What is the average monthly sale?

Average

=

(120 + 150 + 180)

÷

3

=

150

---

- What is the percentage increase from January to March?

Increase

=

180 −120

=

60

Percentage Increase

=

60/120 ×100

=

50%

---

Notice that

the mathematics is simple.

The real challenge is

**reading the information correctly.**

# 10. Why Students Find DI Difficult

Many students think DI is difficult because

❌ They immediately start calculating.

❌ They ignore the graph title.

❌ They miss the units.

❌ They perform unnecessary calculations.

❌ They don't identify what the question is actually asking.

The difficulty is usually **not the mathematics**—

it is **interpreting the information correctly.**

---

# 11. Important Observations

✔ DI is more about analysis than mathematics.

✔ Most calculations involve basic arithmetic.

✔ Reading the graph correctly saves more time than calculating quickly.

✔ Units such as ₹, %, kg, lakh, crore, or millions must always be checked.

✔ Approximation techniques are extremely useful in placement exams.

# 14. Table Data Interpretation

A **Table** organizes information into

- Rows
- Columns

It is the simplest and one of the most common forms of Data Interpretation.

---

## Example

| Year | Sales (₹ Lakhs) |
|------|----------------:|
|2021|120|
|2022|150|
|2023|180|
|2024|210|

Possible Questions

- Highest sales?
- Lowest sales?
- Difference between 2024 and 2022?
- Average sales?
- Percentage increase?

---

## Solved Example

Find the average sales.

Average

=

(120+150+180+210)

÷4

=

660÷4

=

165 Lakhs

↓
