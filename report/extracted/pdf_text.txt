 1. Abstract 
 The  Smart Campus Canteen Management & Food Request Routing System  is a 
 centralized web and mobile application developed to streamline food request management 
 within a college campus. The proposed system addresses the limitations of conventional 
 manual ordering methods by integrating multiple campus canteens, food courts, and 
 hostel messes into a single digital platform. Unlike commercial food delivery 
 applications, the system follows a centralized request-routing model in which food 
 requests are first transmitted to a central college server. The server authenticates users, 
 verifies canteen availability, validates hostel delivery eligibility where applicable, and 
 forwards the request to the selected canteen for processing. Based on current operational 
 conditions, the canteen may accept or reject the request and communicate the estimated 
 preparation time, which is then relayed to the user through the central server. Upon 
 acceptance, the request is fulfilled through either pickup or hostel delivery. 
 The proposed system is designed to serve students, hostel residents, faculty members, 
 staff, canteen operators, and college administrators through a unified platform. It 
 incorporates features such as digital menu management, real-time request tracking, QR 
 code-based pickup verification, secure online payment integration, order history, feedback 
 and rating mechanisms, and an administrative dashboard for monitoring operations and 
 generating analytical reports. By centralizing communication between users and campus 
 food outlets, the system aims to reduce waiting time, improve operational efficiency, 
 minimize congestion during peak hours, and enhance the overall food service experience 
 within the institution. 
 2. Introduction 
 The increasing adoption of digital technologies has transformed service management 
 across educational institutions, including academic administration, library systems, 
 attendance management, and fee payment. However, food service operations in many 
 colleges continue to rely on conventional manual processes, resulting in inefficiencies for 
 both users and canteen operators. Institutions with multiple food courts, departmental 
 canteens, and hostel messes often operate these facilities independently, without a 
 centralized platform for communication, request management, or service monitoring. 
 In the existing system, students, faculty members, and staff are required to physically visit 
 individual canteens to check menu availability, place requests, and wait in queues without 
 any prior information regarding preparation time or crowd levels. Hostel residents face 
 additional inconvenience due to the absence of a structured mechanism for requesting 
 food delivery to their hostels. Consequently, users experience unnecessary waiting, 
 inconsistent service quality, and limited convenience, particularly during peak hours.

 Similarly, canteen operators lack a unified digital platform to manage incoming requests, 
 update menu availability, indicate stock shortages, or regulate request intake during 
 periods of high demand. From an administrative perspective, the absence of centralized 
 monitoring makes it difficult to evaluate canteen performance, analyze service trends, 
 monitor operational efficiency, or generate meaningful reports for decision-making. 
 To address these challenges, this project proposes the  Smart Campus Canteen 
 Management and Food Request Routing System  , a centralized web and mobile 
 application developed exclusively for use within the college campus. Unlike commercial 
 food delivery platforms that facilitate direct transactions between customers and 
 restaurants, the proposed system follows a centralized request-routing architecture. Every 
 food request is first transmitted to a central college server, where it is authenticated, 
 validated, and forwarded to the selected canteen for processing. The canteen then 
 responds with the request status and estimated preparation time, after which the request is 
 fulfilled through either pickup or hostel delivery, depending on the user's preference and 
 service availability. 
 By integrating multiple campus food outlets into a single digital platform, the proposed 
 system aims to improve operational efficiency, reduce waiting time, enhance 
 communication between users and canteen operators, and provide the college 
 administration with a comprehensive platform for monitoring, management, and 
 data-driven decision-making. This report presents the analysis, design, and proposed 
 implementation of the system, highlighting its objectives, architecture, functional 
 components, and anticipated benefits to all stakeholders.

 independent canteens, food courts, and hostel messes, each functioning without a 
 centralized digital platform. As a result, the ordering and service process is largely 
 manual, leading to operational inefficiencies and inconvenience for both users and service 
 providers. 
 Students, faculty members, and staff are required to visit individual canteens to check 
 menu availability, place food requests, and wait in queues without prior knowledge of 
 preparation time or service availability. This often results in increased waiting time, 
 overcrowding during peak hours, and an inconsistent user experience. Hostel residents 
 face additional challenges due to the absence of a structured mechanism for requesting 
 food delivery to hostel premises. 
 From the operational perspective, canteen operators lack an integrated system to manage 
 incoming requests, update menu availability, indicate stock shortages, regulate request 
 intake during busy periods, or communicate estimated preparation times effectively.

 4. Existing System 
 The existing food service system within the college campus primarily operates through 
 conventional manual procedures. Each canteen, food court, or hostel mess functions 
 independently, with users required to visit the respective outlet to view the menu, place 
 food requests, and collect their orders. During peak hours, some canteens may implement 
 token-based or queue-based service mechanisms; however, these systems remain isolated 
 and are not integrated across the campus. 
 The absence of a centralized digital platform makes it difficult for students, faculty 
 members, and staff to access information such as menu availability, estimated preparation 
 time, or operational status before visiting a canteen. Similarly, hostel residents have 
 limited or no access to a formal food delivery mechanism, resulting in inconvenience

 From the service provider's perspective, canteen operators rely on manual order 
 management, making it difficult to regulate incoming requests, update menu availability, 
 communicate preparation delays, or manage customer flow efficiently. Furthermore, the 
 college administration lacks a centralized system to monitor canteen operations, analyze 
 service performance, or generate reports for effective decision-making. 
 Consequently, the existing approach leads to longer waiting times, overcrowding during 
 peak hours, inefficient communication, and limited operational transparency.

 5. Proposed System 
 The proposed system introduces a centralized request-routing architecture in which 
 students, hostel residents, faculty, and staff submit food requests through a single 
 application. Every request passes through a central college server responsible for user 
 authentication, canteen availability verification, and, where applicable, hostel delivery 
 eligibility validation, before being forwarded to the selected canteen. The canteen 
 operator can accept or reject the request, modify the estimated preparation time, mark 
 specific items as unavailable, or temporarily pause incoming requests. Once accepted, the 
 system communicates the estimated preparation time to the requester and sends a 
 notification when the request is ready. The requester can then either collect the food using 
 QR code-based verification or receive it through hostel delivery, where available. 

=== PAGE 6 ===
 This  request-approval model  , rather than an  instant-order model  , is the fundamental 
 architectural distinction between the proposed system and commercial food delivery 
 platforms. It is specifically designed to reflect the operational requirements of campus 
 canteens, where food preparation depends on available resources, current workload, and 
 service capacity rather than guaranteed order fulfillment. 
 5.1 Objectives 
 The primary objectives of the proposed system are: 
 1.  To centralize food request management across all campus canteens, food courts, 
 and hostel messes through a single application. 
 2.  To reduce physical queuing time by enabling advance food requests with real-time 
 status updates. 
 3.  To introduce a structured and eligibility-based hostel delivery mechanism. 
 4.  To provide canteen operators with digital tools for managing food requests, menu 
 availability, inventory status, and operational capacity. 
 5.  To equip the college administration with centralized monitoring, reporting, and 
 analytical capabilities across all campus food service outlets. 
 6.  To improve transparency, accountability, and user satisfaction through request 
 history, feedback, and rating mechanisms.

 8. System Architecture 
 The system follows a layered client-server architecture. At the client layer, three distinct 
 interfaces exist: the Student/Hostel/Faculty/Staff-facing mobile or web application, the 


=== PAGE 10 ===
 Canteen Operator dashboard, and the Administrator dashboard. All client requests pass 
 through an API layer into the Central Server, which houses the core business logic: 
 authentication and role-based access control, request validation, hostel eligibility 
 verification, and routing logic that directs each request to the appropriate canteen. The 
 Central Server interacts with a database layer storing user records, canteen and menu 
 data, request/order records, and feedback data. Two external-facing services support the 
 core logic: a notification service (for push/SMS/in-app alerts at each stage of the request 
 lifecycle) and a payment gateway (for processing in-app payments securely). 
 This layered separation ensures that no client ever communicates directly with a canteen; 
 every interaction is mediated by the Central Server, which is the defining architectural 
 principle of this system and the basis for its centralized monitoring and control 
 capabilities.

 10.2 Level 1 (Decomposed Process View) 
 At Level 1, the single system process is decomposed into five core sub-processes:

 store; 
 (2) Request Validation & Routing, which checks canteen availability and hostel eligibility 
 before forwarding requests to the appropriate canteen; 
 (3) Canteen Response Handling, which processes accept/reject/modify decisions from 
 canteen operators and updates the Request data store; 
 (4) Notification Dispatch, which triggers alerts to users at each stage of the request 
 lifecycle; and 
 (5) Payment Processing, which handles transaction initiation and confirmation with the 
 external Payment Gateway. Three data stores support these processes: the User Database, 
 the Canteen & Menu Database, and the Request/Order Database.

 13. Feasibility Study 
 1.  Technical Feasibility  :  The system relies on well-established, widely supported 
 technologies, including a standard client-server web/mobile architecture, relational 
 or document databases, real-time notification services (such as push notification 
 APIs), and standard payment gateway integrations. No component requires 
 research-grade or experimental technology, making the system technically feasible 
 to develop within an academic project timeline using existing frameworks and 
 libraries. 
 2.  Operational Feasibility  :  Successful operation depends primarily on canteen 
 operator adoption. Each canteen requires at least one operator-facing device and a 
 willingness to shift from manual to digital request handling. Given the relatively 
 small and controlled scope of a single campus, operator training and onboarding 
 are manageable, and a pilot deployment with one canteen can be used to validate 
 the workflow before wider implementation. 
 3.  Economic Feasibility  :  As an internally developed academic and institutional tool, 
 the primary costs include development effort, hosting or server infrastructure, and 
 ongoing maintenance. These costs are significantly lower than licensing a 
 commercial food-delivery platform and eliminate the per-transaction commission 
 fees typically charged by third-party delivery services. The proposed system is 
 therefore economically feasible and justified by reduced recurring costs, improved 
 operational efficiency, and better management of campus food services.

 16. Conclusion 
 The Smart Campus Canteen Management & Food Request Routing System addresses a 
 clear and specific gap in campus food service delivery — one that generic commercial 
 food-delivery platforms are not designed to solve, given their direct-order, open-market 
 model. By introducing a centralized, request-routing architecture mediated by a college 
 server, the system enables structured, transparent, and efficient food service across 
 canteens, food courts, and hostel messes, while giving canteen operators practical tools 
 for demand management and college administration comprehensive oversight through a 
 unified monitoring dashboard. The system architecture, use case, and data flow diagrams 
 presented in this report establish the technical foundation for the subsequent design and 
 implementation phases of the project.
