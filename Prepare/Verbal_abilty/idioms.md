# 14. Business Idioms

Business communication often uses idioms.

Learning them improves both placements and workplace communication.

---

## Hit the Ground Running

Meaning

Start something successfully from the very beginning.

Example

The new software engineer hit the ground running on the first day.

---

## Back to the Drawing Board

Meaning

Start again after failure.

Example

The first product design failed, so the team went back to the drawing board.

---

## Learn the Ropes

Meaning

Learn how something works.

Example

The intern spent two weeks learning the ropes.

---

## In the Loop

Meaning

Well informed about something.

Example

Please keep me in the loop regarding the project.

---

## On the Same Page

Meaning

Having the same understanding.

Example

The development and testing teams are on the same page.

---

## Think Outside the Box

Meaning

Think creatively.

Example

The interviewer asked candidates to think outside the box.

---

## Bottom Line

Meaning

The most important point.

Example

The bottom line is that customer satisfaction has improved.

---

## Win-Win Situation

Meaning

A situation where everyone benefits.

Example

The agreement was a win-win situation for both companies.

---

# 15. Technology & Workplace Idioms

These idioms are common in modern workplaces.

---

## Get the Ball Rolling

Meaning

Start something.

Example

Let's get the ball rolling on the new project.

---

## Pull Your Weight

Meaning

Do your fair share of work.

Example

Every team member should pull their weight.

---

## Ahead of the Curve

Meaning

More advanced than others.

Example

The company stays ahead of the curve by adopting new technologies.

---

## Cut Corners

Meaning

Do something cheaply or carelessly.

Example

Never cut corners while writing production code.

---

## Raise the Bar

Meaning

Set higher standards.

Example

The new product raised the bar for competitors.

---

## Burn the Midnight Oil

Meaning

Work late into the night.

Example

Students often burn the midnight oil before examinations.

---

## Touch Base

Meaning

Contact someone briefly.

Example

Let's touch base tomorrow regarding the presentation.

---

## In Hot Water

Meaning

In trouble.

Example

The employee was in hot water after missing the deadline.

# 17. Context-Based Idiom Questions

Always choose the idiom that best fits the sentence.

---

## Example 1

The coding interview was surprisingly easy.

It was ______.

Options

A. Under the Weather

B. Piece of Cake

C. Spill the Beans

D. In Hot Water

Answer

✅ Piece of Cake

---

## Example 2

Rahul accidentally revealed the surprise party.

He ______.

Options

A. Hit the Ground Running

B. Spilled the Beans

C. Burned the Midnight Oil

D. Learned the Ropes

Answer

✅ Spilled the Beans

---

## Example 3

The startup adopted AI before its competitors.

It stayed ______.

Options

A. In Hot Water

B. Ahead of the Curve

C. Back to the Drawing Board

D. Under the Weather

Answer

✅ Ahead of the Curve

---

## Example 4

After the failed product launch,

the company had to ______.

Options

A. Cut Corners

B. Back to the Drawing Board

C. Break the Ice

D. Miss the Boat

Answer

✅ Go back to the Drawing Board

# 18. Idioms in Reading Comprehension

Sometimes,

Reading Comprehension passages contain idioms.

Understand them through context.

---

## Example

"The CEO encouraged everyone to think outside the box while designing the new application."

Meaning

Think creatively.

---

## Example

"The developer burned the midnight oil to complete the project."

Meaning

Worked late into the night.

---

## Example

"The manager decided to get the ball rolling before the client meeting."

Meaning

Start the work.

---

## Rule

Never interpret idioms literally.

Always use the context.

# 21. Shortcut Techniques

### Shortcut 1

Never translate an idiom word by word.

Understand its figurative meaning.

---

### Shortcut 2

Learn idioms through complete sentences.

---

### Shortcut 3

Group idioms by category.

For example

Success

Failure

Business

Money

Emotions

Technology

---

### Shortcut 4

Read editorials and business articles regularly.

Many commonly used idioms appear naturally.

---

### Shortcut 5

Revise 10–15 idioms every day instead of memorizing hundreds at once.

---

### Shortcut 6

Use newly learned idioms in conversations and writing.

This improves long-term memory.

# 24. Interview Questions

### Q1

What is an idiom?

Answer

An idiom is a group of words whose meaning is different from the literal meaning of the individual words.

---

### Q2

What is the difference between an idiom and a phrase?

Answer

An idiom has a figurative meaning, while a phrase is simply a group of related words that may or may not have a figurative meaning.

---

### Q3

Why should idioms not be translated literally?

Answer

Because their actual meaning depends on usage and context rather than the individual words.

---

### Q4

How can idioms improve communication?

Answer

Idioms make speech and writing more natural, expressive, and fluent.

---

### Q5

How can idioms be remembered easily?

Answer

Learn them with meanings, example sentences, and regular revision instead of memorizing isolated lists.

## Placement Practice

Practice at least

**200–300 high-frequency idioms**

before placements.

For every idiom, learn

- Meaning
- Example Sentence
- Context
- Similar Idioms
- Opposite Meaning (where applicable)

This method is far more effective than memorizing a simple list.
