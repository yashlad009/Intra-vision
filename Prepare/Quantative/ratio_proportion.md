# 1. Introduction

Ratio and Proportion is one of the most fundamental topics in Quantitative Aptitude. It is the backbone for many other chapters such as Percentage, Average, Partnership, Mixture and Allegation, Time and Work, and Time, Speed and Distance.

A **Ratio** is simply a way of comparing two or more quantities of the same kind. It tells us how many times one quantity contains another.

For example, if there are 20 boys and 10 girls in a class, the ratio of boys to girls is written as:

Boys : Girls = 20 : 10 = 2 : 1

A **Proportion** is an equation that states that two ratios are equal.

For example:

2 : 1 = 20 : 10

Both ratios represent the same relationship, so we say they are in proportion.

Because almost every quantitative aptitude chapter uses ratios in some form, mastering this topic early makes every future chapter easier.

# 3. Key Concepts

## 3.1 What is a Ratio?

A ratio compares two quantities of the same unit.

If a and b are two quantities, the ratio of a to b is written as:

a : b or a/b

Here, **a** is called the **antecedent** and **b** is called the **consequent**.

## 3.2 Properties of a Ratio

- A ratio has no unit; it is just a comparison.
- Multiplying or dividing both terms of a ratio by the same non-zero number does not change the ratio.

Example: 4 : 6 = 2 : 3 (dividing both terms by 2)

- A ratio should always be expressed in its simplest form.

## 3.3 Types of Ratios

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| Duplicate Ratio | Ratio of squares | a² : b² |
| Sub-duplicate Ratio | Ratio of square roots | √a : √b |
| Triplicate Ratio | Ratio of cubes | a³ : b³ |
| Compound Ratio | Multiplying two or more ratios | (a:b)×(c:d) = ac:bd |
| Inverse Ratio | Reciprocal of a ratio | b : a (inverse of a:b) |

## 3.4 What is a Proportion?

When two ratios are equal, they form a proportion.

a : b = c : d is written as a : b :: c : d

This is read as "a is to b as c is to d."

Here, **a and d** are called **extremes**, and **b and c** are called **means**.

**Golden Rule of Proportion**

Product of Extremes = Product of Means

a × d = b × c

## 3.5 Continued Proportion

If a : b = b : c, then a, b, c are said to be in **continued proportion**, and b is called the **mean proportional** between a and c.

Mean Proportional (b) = √(a × c)

## 3.6 Third and Fourth Proportional

If a : b = b : x, then x is called the **third proportional** to a and b.

If a : b = c : x, then x is called the **fourth proportional** to a, b, c.

# 5. Memory Tricks

**Trick 1 — LCM Method for Dividing a Quantity**

To divide N in ratio a : b, always add the ratio terms first (a+b), then multiply N by each fraction.

**Trick 2 — Compounding Ratios**

Multiply antecedents together and consequents together.

(2:3) and (4:5) → Compound Ratio = 8:15

**Trick 3 — Cross Multiplication for Proportion**

Whenever four numbers a, b, c, d are given, just check:

a × d = b × c

If true, they are in proportion.

**Trick 4 — Mean Proportional Shortcut**

Mean proportional between two numbers is always the square root of their product — never the average.

**Trick 5 — Simplify First**

Before solving any ratio problem, always simplify the ratio to its lowest form. This avoids large, error-prone numbers later.

# 6. Solved Examples

## Example 1

Divide ₹4,500 between A and B in the ratio 4:5.

**Solution**

Total parts = 4 + 5 = 9

A's share = (4/9) × 4500 = ₹2,000

B's share = (5/9) × 4500 = ₹2,500

**Answer:** A = ₹2,000, B = ₹2,500

---

## Example 2

Find the mean proportional between 4 and 16.

**Solution**

Mean Proportional = √(4×16) = √64 = 8

**Answer:** 8

---

## Example 3

Find the fourth proportional to 3, 6, and 9.

**Solution**

3 : 6 = 9 : x

3x = 6 × 9

x = 54/3 = 18

**Answer:** 18

---

## Example 4

The ratio of ages of A and B is 3:5. If A's age is 21 years, find B's age.

**Solution**

3 parts = 21 → 1 part = 7

B's age = 5 × 7 = 35 years

**Answer:** 35 years

---

## Example 5

If a:b = 2:3 and b:c = 4:5, find a:b:c.

**Solution**

To combine, make the common term (b) equal.

a:b = 2:3 → multiply by 4 → 8:12

b:c = 4:5 → multiply by 3 → 12:15

So a:b:c = 8:12:15

**Answer:** 8:12:15

# 7. Advanced Questions

## Advanced Question 1

The sum of three numbers is 98. If the ratio between the first and second is 2:3, and between the second and third is 5:8, find the second number.

**Solution**

First:Second = 2:3 → multiply by 5 → 10:15

Second:Third = 5:8 → multiply by 3 → 15:24

So First:Second:Third = 10:15:24

Total parts = 10+15+24 = 49

Second number = (15/49) × 98 = 30

**Answer:** 30

---

## Advanced Question 2

A bag contains coins in denominations of ₹1, ₹2 and ₹5 in the ratio 2:3:4. If the total value is ₹560, find the number of ₹5 coins.

**Solution**

Let the number of coins be 2x, 3x, 4x respectively.

Total value = (1×2x) + (2×3x) + (5×4x)

= 2x + 6x + 20x

= 28x

28x = 560

x = 20

Number of ₹5 coins = 4x = 80

**Answer:** 80 coins

**Teaching Note:** In value-based ratio questions, always express each coin's contribution as (denomination × number of coins), then add them to form a single equation in x before solving.

---

## Advanced Question 3

Two numbers are in the ratio 5:7. If 6 is subtracted from each, the ratio becomes 3:5. Find the numbers.

**Solution**

Let numbers = 5x and 7x

(5x−6)/(7x−6) = 3/5

5(5x−6) = 3(7x−6)

25x−30 = 21x−18

4x = 12

x = 3

Numbers = 15 and 21

**Answer:** 15 and 21

---

## Advanced Question 4 (Componendo-Dividendo)

If (3x+4y)/(3x−4y) = 7/3, find x:y.

**Solution**

Using componendo-dividendo:

(3x+4y+3x−4y)/(3x+4y−3x+4y) = (7+3)/(7−3)

6x/8y = 10/4

6x/8y = 5/2

12x = 40y

x/y = 40/12 = 10/3

**Answer:** x:y = 10:3

# 8. Common Mistakes

❌ Adding ratio terms directly without checking if quantities are of the same unit.

❌ Forgetting to simplify the ratio before dividing a quantity.

❌ Confusing mean proportional with simple average — mean proportional uses square root, not (a+b)/2.

❌ Combining two ratios without equalizing the common term first (as shown in Example 5).

❌ Using the wrong total when dividing a quantity — always add ALL parts of the ratio, not just two of them in a three-part ratio.

❌ Ignoring units — a ratio only compares quantities of the same kind and same unit.

# 9. Interview Questions

### Q1. What is the difference between Ratio and Proportion?

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind, while a proportion states that two ratios are equal.

### Q2. Can a ratio have more than two terms?

Yes. A ratio can be extended to three or more terms, such as a:b:c, used often in partnership and mixture problems.

### Q3. What is the mean proportional?

The mean proportional between two numbers a and c is a number b such that a:b = b:c, calculated as √(a×c).

### Q4. Does a ratio have a unit?

No, a ratio is a pure number and has no unit since it is a comparison between two like quantities.

### Q5. What is componendo-dividendo used for?

It is a shortcut algebraic technique used to quickly solve proportion equations involving sums and differences, common in advanced ratio problems.

# 10. Quick Revision

✔ Ratio compares two quantities of the same kind: a:b = a/b

✔ Proportion means two ratios are equal: a:b :: c:d

✔ Product of Extremes = Product of Means (a×d = b×c)

✔ Mean Proportional = √(a×c), NOT the average

✔ To divide N in ratio a:b, use N×a/(a+b) and N×b/(a+b)

✔ Always simplify ratios before solving

✔ To combine ratios, equalize the common term first
