# 1. Introduction

Partnership is one of the most important topics in Quantitative Aptitude because it combines concepts from **Ratio**, **Percentage**, **Profit and Loss**, and **Simple Interest**.

In many placement examinations, Partnership questions are straightforward once you understand one fundamental idea:

> **Profit is shared according to Investment × Time.**

Unlike Profit and Loss, where we calculate profit percentage, Partnership focuses on **how profit should be divided fairly among business partners**.

Because calculations are mostly based on ratios, Partnership is considered one of the highest-scoring aptitude topics.

---

## What is Partnership?

A Partnership is a business arrangement in which **two or more people invest money together** to run a business.

Each person is called a **Partner**.

At the end of the business period,

the total profit (or loss)

is divided among all partners.

But an important question arises.

> Should everyone receive an equal share?

The answer is:

**Not always.**

The share depends on:

- Amount Invested
- Time for which the money remained invested

---

# 6. Golden Rule of Partnership

This is the most important rule of the entire chapter.

> **Profit ∝ Capital × Time**

Read it again.

Profit

is directly proportional to

Investment × Time

This single formula can solve nearly every Partnership question.

---

## Why Does This Rule Work?

Suppose two partners invest the same amount.

Partner A

₹40,000

for

12 months

Partner B

₹40,000

for

6 months

Even though the investment is equal,

Partner A's money worked twice as long.

Therefore,

A deserves twice the profit.

Similarly,

if both invest for the same time,

the person investing more money deserves more profit.

Thus,

both Capital

and

Time

must be considered together.

Hence,

Profit ∝ Capital × Time

 # 11. Time-Based Partnership

Time-Based Partnership is the **most frequently asked concept** in placement examinations.

In these questions,

partners do not invest their money for the same duration.

Some partners:

- Join late
- Leave early
- Increase investment later
- Withdraw money during the business

In such cases,

Profit is **NOT** divided only according to investment.

Instead,

we use the Golden Rule.

> **Profit ∝ Capital × Time**

# 17. Working Partner and Sleeping Partner

A Partnership may contain different kinds of partners.

---

## Working Partner

A Working Partner

- Invests money
- Manages business
- Takes business decisions
- Usually receives Salary or Commission

---

## Sleeping Partner

A Sleeping Partner

- Only invests money
- Does not manage business
- Shares profit according to agreement

---

### Important Point

Salary is usually paid **before** profit sharing.

The remaining profit is divided according to the agreed ratio.

---

# 32. Key Takeaways

• Partnership is based on the principle of **fair profit sharing** according to each partner's contribution.

• If all partners invest for the same time, profit is divided according to the **Capital Ratio**.

• If investment periods are different, profit is divided according to **Capital × Time**.

• The formula **Profit ∝ Capital × Time** is the most important concept in the entire chapter.

• Always simplify ratios before calculating profit shares.

• A partner joining late contributes for fewer months, while a partner leaving early contributes only for the months invested.

• Salary or commission of a Working Partner is generally paid **before** dividing the remaining profit.

• Most campus placement questions can be solved quickly by identifying whether the problem is:
  - Same Time
  - Same Investment
  - Different Time and Investment
