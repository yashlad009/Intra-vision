# 1. Introduction

Percentage is one of the most important topics in Quantitative Aptitude and forms the foundation of many other chapters such as Profit & Loss, Simple Interest, Compound Interest, Data Interpretation, Ratio & Proportion, Mixture & Allegation, and Partnership.

The word **Percentage** is derived from the Latin phrase **"Per Centum"**, which means **"Out of Every Hundred."**

Whenever you see the symbol **%**, simply think:

> "Out of 100"

For example,

- 20% means 20 out of 100.
- 75% means 75 out of 100.
- 125% means 125 out of 100.

Understanding percentages makes calculations easier in real-life situations like discounts, GST, marks, salary increments, election results, sports statistics, bank interest, population growth, and business analysis.

Because of its wide range of applications, almost every placement exam includes questions based on percentages.

# 3. Understanding Percentage

Imagine a classroom has 100 students.

If 40 students are girls,

then

Girls = 40%

because

40 out of 100 = 40%

Now suppose there are only 50 students.

Out of them,

20 are girls.

Percentage of girls

= (20 / 50) × 100

= 40%

Notice that the total number changed,

but the percentage remained the same.

This shows that percentage represents a proportion rather than an actual quantity.

# 5. Basic Formula

The most important formula in this chapter is

Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100

Where

Part = Required Quantity

Whole = Total Quantity

Always remember

The denominator should always represent the total value.

# 14. Percentage Increase

Percentage increase tells us how much a value has increased compared to its original value.

## Formula

Percentage Increase

= ((New Value − Original Value) ÷ Original Value) × 100

Always remember:

The denominator is ALWAYS the **Original Value**.

---

## Example 1

The salary of an employee increased from ₹20,000 to ₹24,000.

Find the percentage increase.

Solution

Original Salary = 20,000

New Salary = 24,000

Increase = 24,000 − 20,000

= 4,000

Percentage Increase

= (4000 ÷ 20000) ×100

=20%

Answer

20%

---

## Example 2

Population increased from 1500 to 1800.

Increase

=300

Percentage Increase

=(300÷1500)×100

=20%

Answer

20%

---

## Shortcut

Step 1

Find the increase.

Step 2

Divide by original value.

Step 3

Multiply by 100.

Never use the new value in the denominator.

---

# 15. Percentage Decrease

Percentage decrease tells us how much a value has reduced compared to its original value.

## Formula

Percentage Decrease

=((Original−New)÷Original)×100

---

## Example

Decrease

=3000

Percentage Decrease

=(3000÷30000)×100

=10%

Answer

10%

---

## Example

Students reduced from 500 to 425.

Decrease

=75

Percentage Decrease

=(75÷500)×100

=15%

Answer

15%

# 16. Increase vs Decrease

Many students confuse these.
