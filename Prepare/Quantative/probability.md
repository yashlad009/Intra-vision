# 4. What is Probability?

Probability is the numerical measure of the chance that an event will happen.

It always lies between

0

and

1.

It tells us

how likely an event is.

The larger the probability,

the greater the chance of occurrence.

---

## Real-Life Example

Suppose you have an umbrella.

You hear that

Probability of Rain

=

0.80

This means

there is an 80% chance that it may rain.

Should you carry the umbrella?

Most probably,

Yes.

Probability helps us make decisions under uncertainty.

# 6. Probability Formula

This is the most important formula of the chapter.

## Formula

Probability

=

Favourable Outcomes

÷

Total Outcomes

Or,

\[
P(E)=\frac{\text{Number of Favourable Outcomes}}{\text{Total Number of Outcomes}}
\]

This formula solves most beginner-level probability questions.

---

 # 14. Complementary Probability

Sometimes, instead of finding the probability of an event occurring,

it is easier to calculate the probability of the event **not occurring**.

This concept is called **Complementary Probability**.

It is one of the most frequently used shortcuts in probability.

---

## Formula

\[
P(\text{Not }A)=1-P(A)
\]

or

\[
P(A')=1-P(A)
\]

Where

- A = Event
- A' = Complement of Event

---

## Memory Trick

Think

> **Either the event happens or it doesn't.**

Therefore,

Probability(Event)

+

Probability(Not Event)

=

1

---

## Example 1

Find the probability of **not getting Head** when a fair coin is tossed.

### Solution

Probability of Head

=

1/2

Therefore,

Probability of Not Head

=

1−1/2

=

1/2

Answer

1/2

---

## Example 2

Find the probability of **not getting 6** on a die.

Solution

P(6)

=

1/6

Therefore,

P(Not 6)

=

1−1/6

=

5/6

Answer

5/6

---

# 15. Addition Rule of Probability

Sometimes,

we need the probability of

**Event A OR Event B**

occurring.

For such questions,

we use the Addition Rule.

---

## Formula

If

A and B are mutually exclusive,

\[
P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)
\]

If they are **not mutually exclusive**,

\[
P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)
\]

---

## Memory Trick

"OR"

↓

Think

Addition

---

=

1/6

P

=

1/6+1/6

=

2/6

=

1/3

Answer

1/3

# 17. Independent and Dependent Events

Understanding these concepts is very important.

---

## Independent Events

Two events are independent

if one event does not affect the other.

Examples

- Tossing a coin twice
- Rolling a die twice
- Spinning a wheel repeatedly

---

### Example

Coin Toss

First Toss

Head

Second Toss

Tail

The second toss is completely independent of the first.

---

## Dependent Events

Events are dependent

if the first event affects the second.

Examples

- Drawing cards without replacement
- Selecting balls without replacement

---

### Example

A bag has

5 Red Balls

5 Blue Balls

If one Red Ball is removed,

the probability of selecting another Red Ball changes.

Hence,

the events are dependent.

---

# 37. Key Takeaways

• Probability measures the likelihood of an event occurring.

• The fundamental formula is:

**Probability = Favourable Outcomes ÷ Total Outcomes**

• Every probability problem begins by identifying the **Sample Space**.

• Complement Probability is useful for solving "at least" and "at most" type questions.

• Use the **Addition Rule** for "OR" events and the **Multiplication Rule** for "AND" events.

• Independent events do not affect each other's probabilities, while dependent events do.

• Memorizing standard sample spaces (coin, dice, cards) helps solve questions quickly.

• Probability is widely used in Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Statistics, Finance, and Data Science.
