# 15. Coordinate Method

One of the fastest methods for solving long Direction Sense questions is the **Coordinate Method**.

Imagine standing at the origin.

```
            North (+Y)
                 ↑
                 |
West (-X) -------O------- East (+X)
                 |
                 ↓
            South (-Y)
```

Starting Point

O = (0,0)

Every movement changes the coordinates.

---

## Rules

Move North

↓

Increase Y

---

Move South

↓

Decrease Y

---

Move East

↓

Increase X

---

Move West

↓

Decrease X

---

## Example

A person walks

5 km North

3 km East

Coordinates become

(3,5)

Answer

Final Position = (3,5)

---

## Advantages

✔ Easy for long questions

✔ Simple distance calculation

✔ Reduces confusion

# 16. Shortest Distance Problems

Many placement questions ask

"How far is the person from the starting point?"

Instead of total distance,

find the **straight-line distance**.

---

## Formula

Using Pythagoras Theorem

```
Shortest Distance

=

√(Horizontal² + Vertical²)
```

---

## Example

A person walks

6 km East

8 km North

Find the shortest distance.

Solution

Horizontal = 6

Vertical = 8

Distance

=

√(6² + 8²)

=

√100

=

10 km

Answer

10 km

---

## Memory Trick

Whenever movement forms a right triangle,

think

**Pythagoras Theorem.**

# 25. Interview Questions

### Q1

What is the difference between total distance and shortest distance?

Answer

Total distance is the complete path travelled.

Shortest distance is the straight-line distance from start to finish.

---

### Q2

Why is the coordinate method useful?

Answer

It simplifies long movement problems and reduces calculation errors.

---

### Q3

What is the biggest mistake students make?

Answer

They forget the current facing direction before taking the next turn.

---

### Q4

Which theorem is commonly used in Direction Sense?

Answer

Pythagoras Theorem.

---

### Q5

How can Direction Sense questions be solved faster?

Answer

Draw a simple diagram, use coordinates when needed, and cancel opposite movements.
